You were right about which part matters

Mechanised from your description, the setup reproduces your own numbers without being told them: 2.1 setups a month (you said once every 10–14 days), a median stop of $226 at five micros (you said $250), and a median reward-to-risk of 6.1 (you said at least 5). Independent reconstruction landing on all three says the structure is right.

Stripped to its geometry — Asia sweep, break of structure, short the −2 extension — it loses money at −0.21R per trade. Add the low volume node requirement and it goes to +1.34R, and at the strictest filter +1.97R with a 42% win rate. Sorted into equal thirds by how empty the entry zone is, expectancy runs +2.53R, +0.16R, −1.02R from emptiest to busiest. That is a dose-response relationship, not a lucky cell.

It rests on 36 trades. That is not enough to call it proven, and section 5 is honest about exactly how not-enough it is. But this is the first thing in three investigations that has looked like a real edge.

01

How I mechanised it

Your chart settled the convention I had wrong. Level 1 sits on the origin, level 0 on the breakout, and the negative levels run past the breakout — so −2.5 is two and a half legs beyond the break, not back behind the origin. I had it inverted, and everything I measured before your screenshot was answering a different question.

  1. Asia range. 00:00–06:00 London, per your 6am close. Qualifies if the last 30 minutes are directionless — Kaufman efficiency ratio ≤ 0.30, which is your "no sense of direction" made countable.
  2. The manipulation. First break of the Asia range after the close. The extreme of that sweep becomes the origin, level 1.
  3. The break of structure. Price must then take out the opposite boundary. That boundary is level 0. Sweep one side, break the other.
  4. Extensions. leg = |0 − 1|; level −k sits k legs beyond the breakout, running away from the origin.
  5. The trade. Limit at −2, stop beyond −2.5, target back at level 1. Stop ≈ half a leg, target ≈ three legs — which is where your 6:1 comes from, and why the 7.42 on your screenshot is the same trade.
Median leg 54 points, median Asia range 50 points. The half-leg stop lands at $226 on five micros, which is how your $250 cap and your 5R minimum turn out to be the same constraint wearing two hats.

03

The node is the edge

I built the volume profile the way you described — from the last time price traded beyond the −2.5 level, forward to the break — and scored the entry zone by its volume relative to the busiest bin in that profile. A low score means a genuine gap in traded volume: the inefficiency you are trying to enter into.

When Pearson is strong and Spearman is flat, the effect is not a reliable ordering — it is carried by a handful of extreme outcomes. Low-volume zones do not produce consistently better trades; they produce the occasional very large winner.

For most strategies that would be a warning. For yours it is the point. At 6:1 you do not need reliable ordering — you need the big winners to cluster where you are looking. They do. This is the same fat-tailed shape I found in the Perera audit, except there the exit design threw the tail away and here your design is built to capture it.

05

Why this is not yet proven

I would rather say this plainly than let the numbers above do more work than they can bear.

  • Thirty-six trades. The tertiles are twelve each. The 42% win rate is nineteen trades. Any of these could move substantially on a handful of different outcomes.
  • No holdout. There is no clean out-of-sample test here, because splitting 36 trades leaves nothing on either side. Every number in this report is in-sample.
  • I chose the thresholds after seeing the data. The tertile split avoids that — it is fixed by construction, and it is the result I would lean on. The 0.15 threshold is fitted and should be discounted.
  • Only 36 of 213 setups could be scored at all. The profile needs price to have traded beyond −2.5 within three sessions. That is itself a selection, and I cannot rule out that it selects for something other than the node.
  • Your discretionary filters are still untested. The 1H/4H reactive zones, order blocks and rejection blocks are not in this model. If they add selectivity on top, the real numbers are better than these. If they are where your judgement quietly does the work, they cannot be automated at all.
And the income arithmetic still does not reach your target. At the 0.25 threshold this produces 0.48 trades a month at about $296 net each on five micros — roughly $142 a month. A real edge, well shaped for a trailing drawdown, and an order of magnitude short of $3,000. Scaling size does not fix it: the stop is already $226 of your $2,000 buffer.
06

What I would do next

  1. Forward-test it, and log every trade. Thirty-six historical trades cannot be turned into ninety by more analysis of the same four years. They can only be added to. Trade it on the demo account, record the LVN score at entry, and in six months there is something real to test. That is the only path from "promising" to "proven" and there is no shortcut.
  2. Multiply instruments, not size. This is the honest answer to the income gap. The edge is rare rather than small, so the fix is more chances at the same risk — ES, YM, RTY, CL, GC all have Asia ranges and volume profiles. Five instruments at 0.5 trades a month each is 2.5 trades a month with the stop still at $226. Correlation between the index products will blunt it, so the metals and energy matter more than the extra equity indices.
  3. Give me the reactive zones. They are the one part of your method still missing, and the part most likely to explain why your live results beat this model. If an order block is definable — a candle's range before a displacement move of some size — I can test whether alignment with the node adds anything. If it turns out not to be definable, that is worth knowing too: it means the edge lives in your judgement and cannot be handed to a bot.

The honest bottom line

Three investigations in, this is the only one where the evidence points the right way. The paper was fabricated, the from-scratch search produced a regime artifact, and your method produced a dose-response relationship on the exact component you said mattered, at a risk profile that actually fits your account. That is a real result and you should take some encouragement from it.

It is also 36 trades, and I am not going to dress that up. Trade it forward, log it, and let the sample decide.
